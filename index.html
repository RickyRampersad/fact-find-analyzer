The Insurance Act, 2018

The obligation was always there. The evidence was not.

Schedule 11 does not ask for a fact find as good practice. It asks for one as a standard of market conduct — taken, retained, and produced on inspection. Page one of every printed fact find carries the words. What changed is that the branch can now produce the evidence for any case, in seconds, without opening a filing cabinet.

Insurance Act No. 4 of 2018 — Schedule 11, B. Understanding Consumers’ Needs
The insurance intermediary or officer should be required to obtain sufficient information about the consumer to ensure an appropriate product is offered. Formal “fact finds” should be specified for long-term savings and investment products and they should be retained and be available for inspection for a reasonable number of years.
“sufficient information about the consumer” Guardian’s Confidential Fact Find V3 in full — eleven sections, with live checks naming whatever is still missing
“to ensure an appropriate product is offered” Every recommendation recorded against the need it answers, with a written reason. A case cannot be approved without one
“formal fact finds should be specified” The company’s own form, section for section — nothing invented, nothing left out
“retained and available for inspection” Every case timestamped and kept: the file, the client’s own confirmation, the manager’s four attestations and their signature
“best suited … given their needs, financial capability, risk profile and specific circumstances” — Market Conduct Guideline The need priced, the premium tested against the client’s actual monthly surplus, and a manager attesting to affordability before anything leaves the branch

Written for one branch. But the obligation belongs to every one of them, and so does the answer.
